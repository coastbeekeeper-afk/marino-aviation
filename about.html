Instructor

Sam Marino, CFI — a professional in the left seat, and in the debrief.

More than six years of flight instruction. A strong pilot with several hundred hours of complex time. A teacher formed by eight CFIs, not one house style.

The flying

Students looking for a top Orange County CFI usually want two things at once: someone who can actually fly the airplane, and someone who can teach. Sam is both.

He brings several hundred hours of complex time and trains in a Cessna 182, so high-performance experience is part of the syllabus rather than a later add-on. John Wayne Airport is not a sleepy Class D trainer field. It is a working airspace. That is the point.

The result is Part 61 training that feels closer to professional aviation than to a weekend hobby mill — without pretending this is an airline factory or a ranked “#1 school.” It is one instructor, held to a high standard, taking a small number of students.

Eight CFIs. One way of teaching.

Sam did not learn to instruct from a single mentor in a single hallway. He trained with eight CFIs. That is a lot of briefings, a lot of conflicting advice, and a lot of chances to see what actually produces a safe, competent pilot.

That pedigree shows up in the lesson: clear objectives, no wasted orbits, and a debrief that names the error instead of cushioning it. If you want a cheerleader, this is the wrong inbox. If you want to get good, write.

Dedicated students with a strong work ethic. That is the filter.

Who this is for

  • Primary students who will study

    Private pilot candidates who treat ground the way they treat the pattern — as real work, not a formality.

  • Ratings with a purpose

    Add-ons and proficiency for pilots who already know what they want from the airplane and need an instructor who will match that intensity.

  • People who can get to SNA

    Training is based at John Wayne Airport, serving Orange County — Santa Ana, Irvine, Newport Beach, Costa Mesa, and the surrounding cities.
